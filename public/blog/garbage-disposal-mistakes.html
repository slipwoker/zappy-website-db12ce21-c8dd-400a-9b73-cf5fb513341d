What Not to Put Down Your Garbage Disposal

June 12, 2025 • 5 min read • By Lone Star Plumbing Co.

Your garbage disposal is one of the most convenient appliances in your kitchen — until it isn't. A jammed, clogged, or burned-out disposal can turn a simple dinner cleanup into a plumbing emergency. The good news? Most disposal problems are completely preventable. Knowing what not to put down your garbage disposal is the single best thing you can do to protect this hardworking appliance and the drain lines behind it.

At Lone Star Plumbing Co., we get called out for disposal issues regularly — and almost every time, the culprit is something that should never have gone down the drain in the first place. Here's the definitive list of what to keep out of your disposal, and why it matters.

Grease, Fats, and Cooking Oils

This is the number one offender. Grease might pour down in liquid form when it's hot, but it cools and solidifies inside your pipes — coating the walls and trapping food debris over time. Even if your disposal handles it fine, the drain line behind it won't. You'll end up with a stubborn blockage that often requires professional hydro-jetting to clear. Instead, pour cooled grease into an old can or jar and toss it in the trash.

Fibrous Vegetables

Celery, asparagus, artichokes, onion skins, and corn husks are all fibrous — and those stringy fibers wrap around your disposal's impeller blades like a tourniquet. The motor strains, the blades jam, and you're left with a disposal that hums but won't spin. Peel and trim fibrous vegetables over the trash can, not the sink.

Eggshells

There's a persistent myth that eggshells sharpen disposal blades. They don't — and the thin membrane inside the shell can wrap around the grinding components just like fibrous material. The crushed shell particles also combine with grease in pipes to form a thick, sandy paste. Compost your eggshells or toss them in the trash.

Coffee Grounds

Coffee grounds feel harmless going down — they're small, they smell fine, and the water seems to flush them away easily. But grounds accumulate in the trap and drain line, building up into a thick, dense sludge. Over time, this is a recipe for a slow drain that turns into a full clog. Compost grounds or dispose of them in the garbage.

Pasta, Rice, and Starchy Foods

Starchy foods expand when they absorb water. Cooked pasta and rice will keep swelling inside your drain lines and disposal trap long after they've gone down. The resulting paste-like blockage is tough to break up and can trap other debris. Scrape plates into the trash before rinsing them at the sink.

Bones and Hard Food Scraps

Chicken bones, fruit pits, popcorn kernels, and nut shells are simply too hard for a standard residential garbage disposal to handle. These items will spin around the grinding chamber repeatedly, dulling the impellers and potentially cracking the disposal housing. Small fish bones are usually okay, but when in doubt, throw it out.

Potato Peels

Potato peels are a silent disposal killer. They pass through the blades easily, but the starchy pulp they create coats the inside of your pipes and forms a thick, sticky film. A large batch of peels can cause an immediate clog. Peel potatoes over a bowl or the trash can — your disposal will thank you.

Non-Food Items

It sounds obvious, but disposals get used as catch-alls more often than you'd think. Twist ties, rubber bands, glass shards, bottle caps, paper, and cigarette butts have all been found in disposals we've serviced. These items cause immediate mechanical damage and can void your warranty. Keep a strainer basket over the drain when the disposal isn't actively in use.

When to Call a Professional

If your disposal is humming without spinning, leaking from the bottom, tripping its reset button repeatedly, or simply won't turn on — it's time to call a plumber. Some issues like a simple jam can be resolved with the hex key tool under the unit, but persistent problems usually signal a worn motor, damaged seals, or a blockage deeper in the drain line that needs professional attention.
