How to Prevent Frozen Pipes During Sudden Texas Cold Snaps

January 14, 2025 · 6 min read · By Lone Star Plumbing Co.

Most Texans don't think much about frozen pipes — and that's exactly the problem. Unlike homeowners in Minnesota or Colorado who winterize every fall without a second thought, Texans are often caught off guard when a sudden Arctic blast pushes temperatures below freezing for days at a time. The result? Burst pipes, flooded homes, and emergency calls flooding our phone lines. We've seen it firsthand after every major freeze event, and we want to help you stay ahead of it.

The good news is that preventing frozen pipes doesn't require expensive upgrades or a full plumbing overhaul. A few smart habits and targeted preparations can save you thousands of dollars in water damage repairs. Here's what every Texas homeowner should know before the next cold snap hits.

Why Texas Pipes Are Especially Vulnerable

Homes built in warmer climates are designed with heat efficiency in mind, not cold resilience. That means pipes are often routed through uninsulated exterior walls, attic spaces, garages, and crawl spaces — areas that plunge rapidly when temperatures drop. Unlike northern states where building codes require insulated pipe runs, Texas construction standards have historically assumed mild winters. The 2021 freeze was a stark reminder of just how exposed our infrastructure really is.

When water freezes inside a pipe, it expands with enormous force — enough to crack copper, split PVC, and blow apart fittings. The real damage often doesn't appear until the pipe thaws and water rushes out of the breach. By then, ceilings, walls, and flooring can absorb hundreds of gallons before anyone notices.

Step 1: Insulate Exposed Pipes Before the Freeze Arrives

Foam pipe insulation sleeves are inexpensive, easy to install, and available at any hardware store. Focus on pipes in unheated areas: under sinks on exterior walls, in the garage, in the attic, and along any exterior wall cavity. Wrap outdoor hose bibs with insulated covers — these are the first points to freeze and one of the most common sources of burst pipes.

For pipes in especially exposed locations, consider adding electric heat tape. This self-regulating tape wraps around the pipe and activates when temperatures approach freezing. It's a worthwhile investment for any pipe that runs through an unheated garage or a drafty crawl space.

Step 2: Keep a Slow Drip Running on Vulnerable Faucets

Running water is much harder to freeze than standing water. On nights when temperatures are expected to drop below 28°F — especially for extended periods — let your most vulnerable faucets drip slowly. Prioritize faucets on exterior walls and those fed by pipes that run through unheated spaces. Even a thin trickle of cold water keeps pressure moving and dramatically reduces freeze risk.

Don't forget to disconnect and drain garden hoses before a freeze. A connected hose traps water in the bib and the supply line behind it, creating a freeze point right at the wall. This is one of the simplest and most overlooked prevention steps.

Step 3: Maintain Indoor Heat — Even When You're Away

If you're leaving town during a cold snap, resist the urge to turn the thermostat down to 55°F to save on your energy bill. Keep your home at a minimum of 65°F. The few extra dollars in heating costs are nothing compared to the cost of emergency pipe repair and water damage restoration. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air to circulate around the pipes.

If your home has a crawl space, make sure vents are closed during a freeze. In warmer months, open crawl space vents promote airflow and prevent moisture buildup — but in a freeze, they let frigid air flow directly over uninsulated pipes below your floor.

Step 4: Know Where Your Main Shutoff Is

Even with every precaution in place, pipes can still freeze and burst. Knowing how to shut off your main water supply immediately can be the difference between a manageable repair and a catastrophic flood. Locate your main shutoff valve now — before you need it. In most Texas homes, it's near the water meter at the street or inside the home near the water heater. Make sure every adult in your household knows where it is and how to use it.

If you suspect a pipe has frozen but hasn't burst yet, turn off the main water supply and call a licensed plumber before attempting to thaw it yourself. Improper thawing — especially with open flame — can cause immediate pipe failure or even a fire. Our team at Lone Star Plumbing Co. is available around the clock for exactly these situations.

When to Call a Professional

If you notice reduced water pressure, no water from a faucet, visible frost on exposed pipes, or wet spots on walls or ceilings after a freeze, call a licensed plumber immediately. These are signs that a pipe may have already frozen or burst. Acting fast limits the damage and gets your home back to normal faster.

Frozen pipe prevention is one of the most cost-effective things a Texas homeowner can do. A few hours of preparation before a cold snap can protect your home from thousands of dollars in damage.
