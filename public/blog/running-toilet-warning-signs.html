When a Running Toilet Is More Than a Minor Annoyance

June 12, 2025 · 5 min read · By Lone Star Plumbing Co.

That faint hiss coming from your bathroom at 2 a.m. might not seem like a big deal. You jiggle the handle, the sound stops, and you head back to bed. But here in Texas, where water bills already climb during the long, dry summers, a running toilet is quietly costing you real money — and it may be signaling a bigger problem lurking inside your tank.

How Much Water Does a Running Toilet Actually Waste?

A toilet that runs continuously can waste anywhere from 200 to over 1,000 gallons of water per day, depending on the severity of the leak. At average Texas water rates, that translates to an extra $30 to $70 on your monthly utility bill — sometimes more. Over the course of a year, a single leaking toilet can cost a household hundreds of dollars in wasted water. If you've noticed your bill creeping upward without an obvious explanation, your toilet is one of the first places we check.

The Most Common Causes Behind a Running Toilet

Most running toilet problems trace back to one of three components inside the tank:

The flapper valve is a rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. Over time, it warps, hardens, or accumulates mineral deposits — especially common in areas with hard water like much of Central Texas. When the flapper doesn't seal completely, water trickles continuously from the tank into the bowl, forcing the fill valve to keep running to compensate.

The fill valve itself can also wear out or become misadjusted. If the float is set too high, water spills into the overflow tube and drains away silently. You may never hear it, but it runs around the clock.

The flush handle and chain are simpler culprits — a chain that's too short, tangled, or caught under the flapper prevents a proper seal after every flush. This is often the easiest fix, but it's also easy to overlook.

When It's More Than a DIY Fix

Replacing a flapper or adjusting a float arm is a reasonable weekend project for a handy homeowner. But if you've already swapped the flapper and the toilet is still running, the problem may run deeper. Cracks in the overflow tube, a failing fill valve assembly, or even a hairline fracture in the porcelain can all cause persistent running. In older homes, corroded shut-off valves behind the toilet can also complicate any repair — attempting to turn them off can cause them to fail entirely, turning a minor fix into an urgent situation.

That's when it's time to call a licensed plumber. At Lone Star Plumbing Co., our technicians carry the parts and tools to diagnose and resolve toilet issues on the first visit. We don't just fix the symptom — we inspect the full toilet system to make sure nothing else is about to give way.

The Hidden Risk: Water Damage

A running toilet that goes unaddressed long enough can do more than inflate your water bill. Constant moisture around the base of the toilet — sometimes caused by a faulty wax ring that a running toilet can accelerate — leads to subfloor rot, mold growth, and tile damage. What starts as a $15 flapper replacement can quietly become a $1,500 subfloor repair if left too long. Texas summers don't help: the combination of heat and humidity creates ideal conditions for mold to take hold fast once moisture gets into the floor structure.

What You Can Do Right Now

Start with the dye test: drop a few food coloring drops into your toilet tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, water is leaking past the flapper. That's your confirmation that something needs attention. From there, assess whether it's a quick part swap or something that warrants a professional eye. When in doubt, a same-day service call from Lone Star Plumbing Co. will give you a clear answer — and peace of mind — fast.
