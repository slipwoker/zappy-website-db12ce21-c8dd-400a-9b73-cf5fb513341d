7 Signs You May Have a Hidden Water Leak

June 12, 2025 · 6 min read · By Lone Star Plumbing Co.

Water is one of the most destructive forces in a home — not because of floods or storms, but because of the slow, silent leaks that go undetected for months. A hidden water leak can rot your walls, buckle your floors, spike your utility bills, and invite dangerous mold growth before you ever notice a single drip. In Texas, where homes face both scorching summers and surprise cold snaps, your plumbing takes a beating year-round.

The good news? Your home usually tells you something is wrong — if you know what to look for. Here are seven warning signs that you may have a hidden water leak somewhere in your plumbing system.

1. Your Water Bill Has Spiked Without Explanation

If your monthly water bill has jumped noticeably and your household habits haven't changed, that extra water is going somewhere. A slow, steady leak from a pipe inside your walls or under your slab can waste thousands of gallons per month. Pull out your last three to six months of bills and look for an upward trend that doesn't match the season or your usage.

2. You Hear Running Water When Nothing Is On

Walk through your home at night when everything is quiet. If you can hear water moving — a faint hiss, a trickle, or a subtle rushing sound — behind walls, under floors, or near the water heater, take it seriously. That sound is water escaping where it shouldn't be. This is one of the clearest early signals of a slab leak or a pressurized pipe failure.

3. Warm or Hot Spots on Your Floor

Feel unusually warm patches on your tile or hardwood floor? A hot water line running beneath your concrete slab may be leaking. The heat from the escaping hot water transfers directly through the slab and into your flooring. This is a classic sign of a slab leak and requires immediate professional attention before structural damage sets in.

4. Visible Mold, Mildew, or Musty Odors

Mold thrives in moisture-rich environments. If you notice dark spots on walls, ceilings, or baseboards — especially in bathrooms, laundry rooms, or along exterior walls — moisture is accumulating behind the surface. Even without visible mold, a persistent musty or earthy smell in a room that should be dry is a strong indicator of hidden moisture from a leak nearby.

5. Stained, Warped, or Bubbling Walls and Ceilings

Water stains that appear as yellow or brown rings on your ceiling or walls are a telltale sign. So is paint that is bubbling, peeling, or blistering without a clear cause. Drywall that feels soft or spongy to the touch has absorbed water — and that water had to come from somewhere. Don't assume it was a one-time event; treat it as an active leak until proven otherwise.

6. Your Water Meter Keeps Moving When You're Not Using Water

Here's a simple test every Texas homeowner should know: turn off every faucet, appliance, and water-using device in your home. Then go outside and watch your water meter for 15 minutes. If the dial or digital display is still moving, water is flowing through your system — and it's going somewhere unintended. This is one of the most reliable DIY methods for detecting a hidden water leak before calling a plumber.

7. Lush, Unusually Green Patches in Your Yard

If one section of your lawn is dramatically greener, thicker, or faster-growing than the rest — even during a dry spell — you may have an underground pipe leak beneath your yard. The escaping water acts as a constant irrigation source for the grass above it. Similarly, soft or soggy ground in a dry climate with no recent rainfall is a strong sign of a buried pipe failure.

What to Do If You Spot These Signs

Don't wait. Hidden water leaks get worse over time, and the longer they go unaddressed, the more expensive the repair becomes. Whether it's a slab leak, a pinhole pipe, or a slow drip behind your drywall, we'll find it fast and fix it right.
